## Narration draft (approximately 110 words; retime to the final recording)

Look closely at this California spiny lobster. Those long antennae and the spiny shell are clues: unlike the familiar clawed lobster, this species has no big front pincers.

NOAA filmed these animals among the rocky reefs of California’s Channel Islands.

By day, California spiny lobsters often shelter in reef crevices. After dark, they emerge to search for food. They are not just scavengers: their diet can include sea urchins, small clams, mussels and worms.

And when danger comes close, a powerful flick of the tail can send a lobster swimming backwards.

A hidden reef resident by day. A night-time forager after dark. That is the California spiny lobster.
