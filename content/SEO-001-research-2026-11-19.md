## Search intent and positioning

The long-form video teaches creators how to turn wildlife footage into a coherent documentary story. It is not a wildlife-animal-facts documentary. Target the creator/editing intent rather than broad viewer searches for wildlife documentaries.

- Primary long-form phrase: **wildlife video editing**
- Secondary phrases: **wildlife storytelling**, **wildlife filmmaking tips**, **documentary storytelling**, **nature documentary editing**, **wildlife videography**
- Short-form phrase: **wildlife video storytelling**
- Long-tail angle: **turn wildlife footage into a documentary story**

**Description (draft; preserve the approved direct-sales disclosure):**

Learn how to turn raw wildlife footage into a clear documentary story. This seven-step guide covers choosing a question, building a beginning-middle-end, editing for meaning, using natural sound, keeping narration factual, creating an honest title and thumbnail, and checking rights and quality before release.

Use only footage you have permission to use. Keep real animal behaviour in its recorded context; never imply an encounter or action that the footage does not show. Keep a respectful distance and never bait, chase or disturb wildlife.
