## Source review

1. **U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service — Ethics & Wildlife Photography**  
   https://www.fws.gov/story/ethics-wildlife-photography  
   Supports the core principle that wildlife welfare and habitat integrity take priority over capturing images, and that filming should be honest and accurate. It describes consulting wildlife biologists and monitoring camera-trap impacts.

2. **Audubon — Guide to Ethical Bird Photography and Videography**  
   https://www.audubon.org/photography/awards/audubons-guide-ethical-bird-photography-and-videography  
   Relevant expert guidance for ethical bird photography/videography. Apply species-specific recommendations only where they actually match the footage.

3. **National Park Service — Respect Wildlife / Leave No Trace**  
   https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/idkt_lnt6.htm  
   States that wildlife should be observed from a distance and not fed, touched, teased, frightened, or intentionally disturbed. Regulations vary by location; do not present a specific legal rule as universal.

## Claim-by-claim treatment

- **“A dramatic wildlife shot is never worth putting an animal at risk.”** Editorial value statement; consistent with the welfare-first guidance above.
- **“Baiting can change an animal's natural behaviour.”** Keep only with qualified wording and credible context. Do not imply every species or baiting method produces identical effects.
- **“It can draw predators toward people, roads, or places where animals may be harmed.”** Needs a species/context-specific source if retained as a factual causal claim. Safer edit: “Baiting can change where animals go and how they behave; follow local wildlife guidance and never lure animals toward people or roads.”
- **“Don't chase an animal, block its escape route, or get closer just to fill the frame.”** Keep as a clear ethical recommendation.
- **“Use a longer lens, stay in a vehicle or at a safe legal distance.”** Use only where a vehicle is lawful and safe. Distance requirements depend on species, location, and local rules; avoid inventing a universal distance.
- **“Record natural sound. Hold the shot.”** Creative advice, not a wildlife science claim.
- **“Responsible wildlife filmmaking isn't about controlling nature.”** Editorial conclusion.
