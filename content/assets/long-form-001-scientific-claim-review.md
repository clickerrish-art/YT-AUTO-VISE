## Result

**PARTIAL PASS — the principal sensory, ecology, locomotion, feeding and human-safety claims are supported by the sources checked, with scope qualifications.** No changes were made to the owner-approved narration. The script correctly avoids claiming supernatural detection range or that every ripple triggers an attack. Two important boundaries must remain explicit in narration and visuals:

1. The 2015 wave-threshold work used conditioned animals and controlled, single-frequency surface-wave stimuli. It establishes sensitivity under experimental conditions, not a universal field detection range or the ability to identify prey species from any distance.
2. The 2025 temporal-integration study tested young Nile crocodiles responding to controlled airborne sound and water-surface vibration sources. It does not directly observe or prove the sequence of decisions during a wild prey hunt.

## Claim-by-claim review

| Script claim | Evidence checked | Assessment and production constraint |
|---|---|---|
| Eyes, nostrils and ears can be positioned at the air-water interface while the rest of the body remains submerged | Caron Delbosc et al. (2025), Introduction, lines 186–190 of the PMC article | Supported as a documented crocodilian ambush posture. Visuals must show the correct head anatomy and must not imply every crocodile always adopts this posture. |
| Integumentary sensory organs (ISOs) detect surface-water disturbance and underwater displacement | Caron Delbosc et al. (2025), Introduction; Leitch & Catania (2012) | Supported. Call them sensory organs/mechanoreceptors; do not animate them as visible radar, sonar beams or glowing sensors. |
| Nile crocodiles are highly sensitive to small surface waves under experimental conditions | Grap et al. (2015), PubMed abstract | Supported. The study conditioned Nile crocodiles to respond to controlled surface waves at 15, 30, 40, 60 and 80 Hz. Reported peak-to-peak thresholds ranged from 10.3 μm at 15 Hz to 0.5 μm at 80 Hz. Do not turn these laboratory thresholds into a wild detection-distance claim. |
| Crocodiles integrate sound and water-surface vibration information over time | Caron Delbosc et al. (2025), Abstract, Results and Discussion | Supported with qualification. In controlled tests, young Nile crocodiles preferentially approached the water-wave source over an airborne-sound source when the stimuli were presented within the tested timing window. Do not describe this as proof of human-like reasoning or a fully mapped wild hunting decision. |
| ISOs occur on the Nile crocodile's body as well as its head | Leitch & Catania (2012); Caron Delbosc et al. (2025) | Supported for crocodilids; distribution differs among crocodilian families. Avoid using an alligator-only body diagram for a Nile crocodile. |
| Detection is not the same as identification or an automatic strike | Interpretation consistent with the experimental scope above | Appropriate editorial qualification. Keep it; do not replace with certainty that a ripple identifies prey or guarantees an attack. |
